privacy ✿

last updated 11 November 2026

Scribbles is a small, personal app for lists, recipes, notes and monthly recaps. It’s built to keep your things yours. Here’s exactly what happens to them.

if you don’t sign in

Everything you make stays on your device, saved in your browser’s own storage. Nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing your browser’s data for the site removes it.

if you sign in

Signing in lets you see your things on all your devices. To do that, Scribbles keeps:

  • your account: your email address, and a password if you made one (stored scrambled, never readable). If you sign in with Google, Google tells Scribbles your email address and name, and that’s all.
  • what you make: your lists, recipes, notes, monthly pages, photos and drawings, so they can sync.

These are stored with Supabase, the service that runs Scribbles’ database and storage. They’re locked so that only your account can read or change them.

what Scribbles doesn’t do

  • no ads, and no tracking or analytics
  • your things are never sold, shared or used for anything except showing them back to you
  • no one else signing in to Scribbles can see your things

other services the app loads

  • Google Fonts supplies the handwritten fonts, so Google sees that your device loaded them.
  • A hidden easter egg plays a YouTube video, using YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, only if you go looking for it.

deleting your things

Anything you delete in the app is deleted from your account too. To delete your whole account and everything synced with it, ask here and it’ll be done.

changes

If this ever changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change with it.
